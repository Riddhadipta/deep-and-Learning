🔀 Slide 10: Interactive Computation Graph

Slide 10 decomposes complex functions into a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of elementary operations:

f(a, b) = (a + b) \cdot (b + 1)

Reverse-Mode Gradient Accumulation (Slide 10 & 11)

Analytical Backprop Derivation:
$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial e} = 1.0$$
$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial c} = d = 2.0, \quad \frac{\partial e}{\partial d} = c = 3.0$$
$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial a} = \frac{\partial e}{\partial c} \frac{\partial c}{\partial a} = 2.0 \times 1 = 2.0$$
$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial b} = \frac{\partial e}{\partial c}\frac{\partial c}{\partial b} + \frac{\partial e}{\partial d}\frac{\partial d}{\partial b} = 2.0(1) + 3.0(1) = 5.0$$
💡 The Branch-Sum Rule (Slide 9 & 10)

When variable $b$ fans out into multiple intermediate nodes ($c$ and $d$), its total derivative sums the contributions along all downstream paths: $\frac{\partial e}{\partial b} = \frac{\partial e}{\partial c}\frac{\partial c}{\partial b} + \frac{\partial e}{\partial d}\frac{\partial d}{\partial b}$.

💥 The Linear Collapse Theorem (Slide 30)

What happens when you chain multiple linear layers without activation functions?

\begin{aligned} y &= W_3(W_2(W_1 x + b_1) + b_2) + b_3 \\ &= W_3 W_2 W_1 x + (W_3 W_2 b_1 + W_3 b_2 + b_3) \\ &= W_{\text{effective}} x + b_{\text{effective}} \end{aligned}

Toggle between a Linear Activation ($\phi(a)=a$) and a Non-linear Activation (ReLU/Sigmoid). Watch what the 3-layer network can separate!

Interactive Decision Space

🚨 Linear Collapse Active

Because $\phi(a)=a$, the 3 layers mathematically collapsed into a single straight hyper-plane. It cannot solve non-linear problems like XOR!

Optimization, Losses & Empirical Risk Minimization

Derive gradients, compare Squared Error vs Cross-Entropy, and step through Slide 39's gradient descent algorithm.

Linear Regression with Housing Data (Slide 11 & 39)

\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial W_j} L(W) &= (f_W(x^i) - y^i) x_j^i \\ W_j &\leftarrow W_j - \alpha (f_W(x^i) - y^i) x_j^i \end{aligned}

Loss Surface Contour $L(W, b)$ & Math Trace

Algorithm Iteration Trace (Slide 40):
Sample: Living Area $x = 1800 \text{ ft}^2$, True Price $y = \$380\text{k}$
Predicted $f_W(x) = \$320\text{k}$
Residual Error $(f_W(x) - y) = -60$
Gradient contribution = $-60 \times x_j$
Updated $W$: $W_{\text{new}} \leftarrow W_{\text{old}} - \alpha \times \text{grad}$

🎯 Deriving Cross-Entropy from Likelihood (Slides 17–20)

Slide 18 unifies the two Bernoulli outcomes into a single exponential likelihood:

P(y^i | x^i, W) = g(f_W(x^i))^{y^i} \cdot [1 - g(f_W(x^i))]^{1 - y^i}

Taking negative log converts maximum product to minimum sum (Cross-Entropy):

\min_W - \sum_{i=1}^n [y^i \log(g) + (1 - y^i) \log(1 - g)]

Cross-Entropy vs MSE Loss Curve

0.162 Cross-Entropy Loss
0.011 Squared Error Loss
Steep (Fast) Gradient Signal

Notice: When the model is confidently wrong ($\hat{y} \to 0$ for $y=1$), Cross-Entropy loss explodes to $\infty$ providing strong gradient flow, whereas MSE flattens out due to the derivative of sigmoid!

🐱 Multi-Class Output & Softmax (Slide 34–36)

From Slide 34: The network outputs vector logits for multiple classes: {cat, dog, car}.

\text{Softmax}(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=0}^{|c|-1} e^{z_j}}

Normalized Class Probabilities & Loss

L = - \sum_{j=0}^{|c|-1} c[j] \log(p_j)
